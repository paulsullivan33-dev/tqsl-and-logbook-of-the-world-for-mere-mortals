# The neighbours

Outcome: you can place LoTW among the other confirmation systems
a radio amateur meets, and say what each one trusts instead of a
signature.

**eQSL.cc** is the oldest electronic alternative: a website
where both stations upload logs and confirmations appear as
electronic cards you can view and print. Its trust model is the
account login rather than a per-user cryptographic identity, so
its confirmations feed its own award programme rather than the
ARRL's. Many operators use both: LoTW for ARRL awards, eQSL for
its cards and its own awards.

**QRZ Logbook**, attached to the QRZ.com callsign database, is a
web logbook first and a confirmation system second. It will
import from and export to LoTW, so a station can keep its log on
QRZ and still feed ARRL awards. Where TQSL signs on your
machine, QRZ's confirmations are made inside its own service —
the convenience is real, and so is the difference in what is
being trusted.

**Club Log** is built for clubs, expeditions and analysis: it
digests a club's combined logs, produces league tables and
propagation-flavoured reports, and can push QSOs onwards to
LoTW. Expedition stations in particular often appear on Club Log
long before their LoTW upload lands.

**Paper QSL cards** are the original system LoTW digitised, and
they have not vanished: cards still travel direct by post and
through the **QSL bureaus**, and some awards and some operators
still prefer them. A card needs no account, no certificate and
no software — and takes months, costs postage, and proves rather
less about who sent it. Plenty of stations run paper and LoTW in
parallel, for different partners.

**Your logging program** is the neighbour you already live with.
It records QSOs and exports the ADIF file TQSL signs. Some
loggers call TQSL for you behind a menu item; that convenience
changes nothing in this guide — the certificate, the location
and the signature are still TQSL's, and the Final Status is
still the receipt worth reading.

**The check.** One question sorts the neighbourhood: *what makes
a confirmation from this system believable?* For LoTW, a key the
ARRL issued after a postal identity check. For the web services,
an account. For a paper card, the postmark and the card itself.
Choose per award and per partner; the systems coexist because
they answer that question differently.
